## Match the bottle to the problem, not to the brand

Neutrogena's marketing tends to present these as one family. They behave like four different products.

**Flaking with itch, and you can live with a medicinal smell.** T/Gel Therapeutic Original. Coal tar slows down the rapid skin-cell turnover that produces flakes, which is why it also gets used for seborrhoeic dermatitis and scalp psoriasis, not just dandruff. The US label is explicit that it can temporarily discolour grey, blonde, bleached or tinted hair, so if you have highlights, keep it off the lengths.

**Flaking so heavy that scale is actually building up.** T/Sal, with 3% salicylic acid. Salicylic acid works differently from coal tar: it breaks down the scale that's already sitting there rather than slowing new cell production. That's also why it's the one people reach for when their scalp feels crusty rather than simply itchy.

**Severe, red, intensely itchy patches.** T/Gel Extra Strength carries 1% coal tar, double the concentration of the original. The label says not to use it for prolonged periods without consulting a doctor, and to check with one before use if the affected area covers a large part of your body. Take that literally.

**Dry or tight scalp with no real dandruff.** Here the medicated options are the wrong tool. Healthy Scalp Hydro Boost and Soothe & Calm are non-medicated, pH-balanced, free of sulfated surfactants, SLS and SLES. They cleanse and moisturise. They will not treat psoriasis, and reading a review complaining that they "didn't cure my dandruff" is a category error.

**Oily scalp and build-up from styling products.** Pink Grapefruit Clarify & Shine, which is aimed at residue and shine rather than medicated treatment.

**You want salicylic acid but can't tolerate fragrance or sulfates.** Scalp Therapy Anti-Dandruff Extra Strength is the 3% salicylic acid option that's fragrance-free and free of parabens and sulfates, and it's labelled for daily use, which is unusual in this category.

## T/Gel vs T/Sal, since that's the comparison everyone ends up making

Both are used at least twice a week, or as directed. Both ask you to wet your hair, work the lather into the scalp, leave it there for several minutes, then rinse and repeat. That leave-on step is the one people skip, and it's the one that lets the active ingredient do anything at all.

The difference is the mechanism. Coal tar in T/Gel slows skin-cell turnover, which reduces how much flaking happens in the first place. Salicylic acid in T/Sal dissolves existing scale, which is faster on build-up but doesn't address the underlying turnover. In practice, some people use T/Sal to clear the scale first and then maintain with T/Gel.

Neither one is a daily habit for most people. A pharmacy-backed buyer's guide on the T/Gel range suggests most people only need it two or three times a week, that some notice improvement after one or two washes while persistent problems take two to four weeks of regular use, and that daily washing with it can be drying on some hair types. Alternate with a gentle shampoo if your hair starts feeling straw-like.

## What the reviews actually complain about

One hair-care publication's round-up reports T/Gel sitting at an average of around 4.6 out of 5 across more than 16,000 retail reviews, with 75% of them five stars. That number is their aggregation, not a figure Neutrogena publishes, so treat it as directional.

The pattern inside the reviews is more useful than the average. People who get relief tend to mention it working after one wash, and then keep mentioning the smell. Aggregated customer reviews on the T/Gel Original listing run from "so good I hope they never change the formula" to "works wonderful, smells awful," and the smell complaint is specific: coal tar has a medicinal, ashy note that lingers in hair for a while after rinsing. That is a real trade-off and it doesn't go away with the extra-strength version.

If you know the smell will stop you using it consistently, the rational move is Scalp Therapy or T/Sal, both of which skip coal tar. An inconsistent routine with the "best" product beats nothing.
